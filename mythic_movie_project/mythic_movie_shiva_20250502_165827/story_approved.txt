In the heart of the Himalayas, where clouds kiss icy peaks, a figure sits unmoved—bare-chested, ash-smeared, wrapped in tiger skin. Shiva. His matted hair coils like serpents, cradling the Ganga as it cascades from the heavens. The crescent moon gleams on his brow, while his third eye pulses with silent fire. Around him, the world stills. Snowflakes hover mid-air, afraid to land. A damaru beats in his palm—each rhythm birthing time, each silence ending a cycle. Nandi waits nearby, unmoving, a statue carved by devotion. Suddenly, a serpent slithers across Shiva's shoulders, hissing into the void. With a breath, Shiva opens his eyes. Storms rise. Galaxies ripple. And just as suddenly, he closes them. Silence returns. Creation rests again in the calm between his breaths.
